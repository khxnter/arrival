Making Wine

Based on roughly 1.5 kg of grapes, makes about 2 litres at around 12–13% alcohol. Sterilise everything that touches the wine (bowl, spoon, sieve, bottles) with a no-rinse steriliser or Campden solution.

🍇 Day 1 — Prepare the grapes

  • Remove all stalks and discard any mouldy, rotten or damaged grapes.
  • Give the grapes a quick rinse with cold water.
  • Weigh the grapes after removing the stalks — that's the useful weight.
  • Put them in a large sterilised bowl or bucket.
  • Crush them thoroughly with clean hands or a potato masher. You want the skins broken and plenty of juice released.
  • Don't blend them — that will smash the seeds and can make the wine bitter.

🍬 Add sugar

  • Measure 150–200 g white granulated sugar. Use the lower end for sweet supermarket grapes and the higher end for less sweet garden grapes.
  • Dissolve it in about 200–300 ml of boiled water.
  • Allow the sugar water to cool to room temperature.
  • Add it to the crushed grapes.
  • Add enough additional cooled boiled water to give you roughly 2 litres of total mixture.
  • If you have a hydrometer, measure the juice before adding sugar, then add sugar until it reads about 1.085–1.090.

🧫 Add the yeast

  • Use Lalvin EC-1118.
  • For this amount, about 1 g of yeast is plenty (a 5 g sachet is meant for up to about 20 litres).
  • Sprinkle it over the mixture and gently stir it in.
  • Don't add the yeast while the liquid is hot — it can kill it.

🫧 Days 1–5 — First fermentation

  • Cover the container with a clean cloth/muslin secured with an elastic band, or use a loose lid that allows gas to escape.
  • Keep it somewhere around 18–24°C, out of direct sunlight.
  • Stir once or twice a day with a thoroughly sterilised spoon.
  • Push the floating grape skins down into the liquid when you stir.
  • You should start seeing bubbles/foaming within roughly 12–48 hours.

🍷 Around Day 5 — Strain

  • Sterilise a sieve or muslin and a glass bottle/demijohn that the wine will nearly fill. For ~2 L of wine, use a 2 L container, not a 5 L demijohn — too much air space lets the wine oxidise over the longer fermentation.
  • Pour the grape mixture through the sieve or muslin.
  • Squeeze the grapes/skins to extract as much juice as possible.
  • Discard the skins and seeds.
  • Pour the strained wine into the bottle, filling it up to the shoulder/neck.

🫧 Days 5–30+ — Main fermentation

  • Fit an airlock to the container.
  • Keep it around 18–22°C.
  • Leave it alone to ferment.
  • You'll see bubbles coming through the airlock.
  • Don't refrigerate it during fermentation.
  • When bubbling has stopped, leave it for another week or so rather than bottling straight away. The wine should start to clear and a layer of sediment will settle at the bottom.
  • If you have a hydrometer, fermentation is finished when the reading stays the same (around 0.990–0.996) for 2–3 days in a row.

🍾 Bottling

  • Once fermentation has genuinely finished, carefully siphon/pour the clear wine into sterilised bottles, leaving the sediment behind.
  • Do not bottle while it's still actively fermenting — pressure can build up and bottles can burst.
  • Seal the bottles.
  • Let it mature somewhere cool and dark.
  • EC-1118 ferments almost all the sugar, so expect a dry wine.

⏳ When can you drink it?

  • ~1 month: drinkable, but probably rough.
  • 2–3 months: should be noticeably better.
  • 6+ months: potentially considerably smoother.
